# The 4-minute demo arc (staged version)

For live audiences: the presenter (or a volunteer) pitches Dana. Three planted
moments make the demo land even if improvisation wobbles. Rehearse them; the
rest can be natural.

## Setup on stage
- One laptop, headphones ON (keeps buyer TTS out of the mic → clean analysis).
- Main screen: the call UI only. Nothing analytical visible.
- Optional second screen (presenter-only, revealed at the end): live signal feed.

## The three planted moments

**1. The pricing flinch (~1:30).** Dana asks "what does this cost?" The
presenter deliberately hesitates: "It's, uh... well, it depends, but, um,
around three thousand a month." → Dana probes the hesitation. Audience notices
Dana *heard* something.

**2. The ramble (~2:30).** Presenter launches into a 45-second feature
monologue. → Dana audibly disengages: flat "mm-hm", then "okay — so anyway".
Audience recognizes the universal experience of losing a buyer.

**3. The recovery (~3:15).** Dana raises the "we tried a tool in 2024"
objection. Presenter answers short, confident, concrete. → Dana concedes and
warms up: "Fair. That actually answers it. Send me the pilot terms."

## The payoff (~4:00)
Presenter ends the call. Dana breaks character:

> "Okay — stepping out of Dana for a second. Two things I picked up. When I
> asked about price, you paused and your delivery went flat — buyers hear that
> as 'the number is negotiable' or 'they're hiding something'. And around the
> two-thirty mark I checked out completely — you were listing features and I'd
> stopped responding, but you didn't change course for another forty seconds."

Then the kicker line to the audience: **"Everything Dana just did — the
probing, the checking out, the debrief — came from audio alone. No camera. One
API."** (Optionally reveal the presenter screen's signal feed here.)

## Self-serve variant
Same app, no script: visitors pitch Dana for 3 minutes and get their own
debrief. Robustness > drama: cap session length, handle silence gracefully,
debrief works even with sparse signals ("honestly, you held steady the whole
call — here's the one moment worth a look...").
